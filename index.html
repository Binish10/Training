1  Daily structure

Two fixed blocks. Walking in the morning, training in the evening.

Equipment 6 kg and 10 kg kettlebells · push-up board · 3 ft loaded rod · agility ladder and cones · resistance parachute · pull-up bar · resistance bands

IncidentalThrough the dayCalls, errands, moving around~1,500
Evening6:00 pmWarm-up → session → cool-down → 25 min walk~3,500

Training sits in the evening because body temperature, joint compliance and peak power output all peak in the late afternoon and bottom out in the early morning. For sprinting and jumping that gap is the difference between a good session and a pulled hamstring.

Dinner comes after the evening block. Eat something light around 4:30 pm if lunch was early — kettlebell complexes and sprints go badly on an empty tank.

Why Friday holds everything explosive Sprints, plyometrics and cone agility are the same quality of work — maximum output, short bursts, full recovery. Grouping them gives Saturday and Sunday a real 48-hour window for tendon recovery instead of scattering low-grade impact across the whole week.
Weeks 1 and 2: no Friday session. Running, sprinting and the step ramp all land on the same calves, Achilles and plantar fascia. Starting all three at once is the most reliable way to pick up shin splints in week three. Walk on Friday instead, and add the speed day from week 3, once running is established.
Friday morning is the walk to keep short and flat. Sprints need calves that haven't already done 5,000 steps.

3  The five sessions

Rest 90 seconds between sets. Every set ends one to two reps short of failure.

Monday — Upper body and hinge

ExerciseSets × repsNotes
Pull-ups4 setsLevel table below. Hardest thing first, while fresh.
Rod overhead press3 × 8Before push-ups, so shoulders fail before triceps do
Rod bent-over row3 × 10Flat back, pull to the navel
Push-up board3 sets, two gripsAlternate wide (chest) and narrow (triceps) slots
Rod RDL3 × 10Moved here from Wednesday — see below
Band face-pulls or Y-T-W raises2 × 12–15Direct counter to desk posture — don't skip
Why the RDL lives here now It's the plan's most eccentric hamstring work, and eccentric soreness peaks 24–48 hours later. On Wednesday that put it exactly two days before Friday's sprints — the session that most punishes sore hamstrings, and the one where hamstrings tear. Monday to Friday gives four days instead of two. It also fixes a grip problem: on Wednesday the RDL followed a complex ending in rows, so your hands gave out before your hamstrings did.

Order matters more than usual here. Pull-ups first while the nervous system is fresh, then the overhead press, then rows, then push-ups. Pressing before push-ups means the shoulders reach their limit before the triceps do — the other way round and the press is capped by already-tired triceps.

If you don't have bands yet Face-pulls, band-assisted pull-ups, pull-aparts and shoulder dislocates all assume a resistance band set. A set costs ₹500–800 and is the highest value-per-rupee addition to this kit. Until then: Y-T-W raises replace face-pulls, a towel replaces the band for dislocates, and band-assisted pull-ups become negatives.

Pull-up progression

Rotate grips week to week — overhand, underhand, neutral. This spreads the load across different angles of the back and reduces repetitive elbow strain.

Until the pull-up bar arrives Substitute rod bent-over rows 4 × 10, single-arm kettlebell rows 3 × 10 per side, and inverted rows under a sturdy table for 3 sets to near-max reps.

Wednesday — Kettlebell and legs

Why squats had to be added The goblet squat inside the complex is loaded by the weakest movement in the chain — the overhead press — so it will always be far too light to train your legs. Wednesday is the lower-body day and you own a loaded rod. Front squats give you a real bilateral squat; split squats train one leg at a time, which matters because running and sprinting are single-leg activities and side-to-side asymmetry is a common route into knee and hip trouble.
The trade-off, stated plainly. Squatting on Wednesday means some quad soreness on Friday's jumps. That's a milder risk than the hamstring one it replaces, and it's manageable if you keep squats two reps shy of failure. If your legs feel heavy on a given Friday, cut the plyometric volume that week rather than skipping sprints.
The weight rule Choose the bell by the weakest movement in the chain — almost always the overhead press. If you can't press it clean at the end of a round, it's too heavy for the whole complex.

Complex 1 — Strength, 10 kg

All reps on one arm, then switch. Four rounds per side, 90–120 seconds between rounds.

Complex 2 — Conditioning, 6 kg

Continuous, one side then the other. Four rounds per side, 60–90 seconds between rounds.

Complex 3 — Mixed, 10 kg and 6 kg

Ten racked at the chest, six overhead. Asymmetric squat × 5 → racked carry 20 m → switch sides. Three rounds.

Progression. Add a round before adding reps. At six clean rounds, extend the complex by one movement rather than piling on volume.

Why the rod matters Ten kilos caps out quickly on squats and hinges. Front squats and split squats here, RDLs and rows on Monday, are what will actually build your legs, posterior chain and back — the bells alone can't load those patterns heavily enough for long.

Thursday — Active recovery

No load. Sixty-minute morning walk, then ten minutes of mobility in the evening: cat-cow, bird-dog, world's greatest stretch, hip circles, thoracic rotations. Follow with Cool-down C, holding each position 45–60 seconds instead of 20–30.

This day isn't filler. It's what makes five training days sustainable.

Friday — Speed day

Fixed order. Each element is more neurally demanding than the last is forgiving, so the sequence isn't negotiable.

1 · Ladder — 4 minutes

Two-feet-in, lateral shuffle, in-in-out-out, Icky shuffle. Each pattern down and back twice. Fast feet, light contacts, no stomping. Floor patterns are in section 6.

2 · Parachute sprints — the main event

Distance30 m
Reps6, building to 8
Rest90 sec — walk back and then some
EffortNear-maximum
Space40–60 m of clear ground. A park or maidan, not a street.
Three rules for the parachute Find the quick-release toggle before your first rep. Check the wind — a headwind turns moderate drag into heavy drag, a tailwind collapses the canopy, so run across it if it's gusty. And never use the parachute for distance running; sustained drag pulls you into a forward lean that damages your stride mechanics.

When your reps visibly slow, the session is over. A fatigued sprint teaches your body to move slowly.

Alternate weeks: plyometrics or cones, never both. The plan's own rule is never to do explosive work fatigued — and stacking sprints, then 80 jumps, then eleven maximal cone shuttles breaks it twice over. Change of direction is a skill, and skills degrade first, so the cone work was always being done tired. Odd weeks: sprints plus plyometrics. Even weeks: sprints plus cones.

3a · Plyometrics — odd weeks — 60 to 80 total foot contacts

Land quietly. Absorb through the hips, not the knees. Full reset between reps, 60–90 seconds between sets.

3b · Cone agility — even weeks — 8 minutes

T-drill × 4, 5-10-5 shuttle × 4, box drill × 3. Full recovery between each, 45–60 seconds. Patterns in section 6.

Session total including warm-up and cool-down: about 50 minutes.

Static holds, 20–30 seconds per side. Inhale four seconds, exhale six — the long exhale is what actually shifts you into recovery. Go to mild tension, never sharp pain. Rehydrate straight after.

Hold the ramp even though it feels easy. You're adding sprints, plyometrics and two runs at the same time — four new sources of load on the same calves, Achilles and plantar fascia. Tendon tissue adapts weeks slower than muscle, which is why shin and heel problems tend to appear around week three, when you already feel fine.

Adjustments by day

  • Tuesday and Saturday — the run contributes 2,500–3,000 steps, so the morning walk drops to 30 minutes.
  • Thursday and Sunday — go to 60 minutes and catch up on anything you're behind on.
  • Friday — short and flat in the morning, easy and unhurried in the evening. Never brisk on sprint legs.

Practical notes

  • Morning stiffness — spend the first five minutes at an easy pace before settling into anything brisk. Ankles and Achilles are least compliant in the first hour after waking.
  • Fasted walking is fine at this intensity. Water before you head out.
  • Pace — mostly easy. Two or three days a week, make 20 minutes brisk enough that talking becomes slightly effortful.
  • Shoes — at roughly 70,000 steps a week, footwear is the single biggest factor in whether your feet hold up. Replace at 600–700 km.

8  Progression and rules

How each thing progresses

ElementRule
Pull-upsAdvance a level only when all four sets are strict with 1–2 reps still in reserve.
Push-upsAt 30 clean standard reps, stop adding reps. Move the board grips to harder slots, elevate the feet, or add a 2-second pause. Past 30, more reps builds endurance, not strength.
KettlebellsAdd a round before adding reps. At six clean rounds, extend the complex by one movement.
Rod workAdd weight when all sets finish with 2 reps in reserve. Small jumps.
Split squatsProgress the weaker side first, then match the stronger side to it. Never let the gap widen.
SprintsSix reps building to eight over four weeks. Don't add distance — 30 m is right for pure acceleration.
PlyometricsHold at 60–80 foot contacts for the first month. Increase after that, and only to 100.
RunningFollow the run-walk table. Don't jump stages early because one session felt easy.
WalkingSix-week ramp to 10,000. This is the one to be most patient with.

Non-negotiables

Sequence within a session. Agility and plyometrics come first, while the nervous system is fresh. Then kettlebells or heavy rod work. Bodyweight work last. Never do explosive work fatigued.

Forty-eight hours between hard sessions for the same system. This is why Thursday and Sunday exist, and why Friday is consolidated into one speed day.

Stop short of failure. Most sets end one or two reps before you'd fail. Taking pull-ups to absolute failure repeatedly is the fast route to elbow tendonitis.

Land quietly. Every jump, hop and bound. Noise means you're absorbing impact through joints instead of muscle.

Full range of motion. Dead hang to chin over the bar. Chest near the floor on push-ups. Partial reps build poor mechanics and less strength.

Posture work is main work. Face-pulls, rows and the lowering phase of pull-ups are the direct counter to hours at a desk. Treat them as part of the session, not optional accessories.

When to back off Sharp pain rather than muscle soreness. A sprint session where times drop off after three reps. Resting heart rate elevated several days running. Take an extra recovery day — the plan survives a missed session, it doesn't survive an injury.
